## Pakistan has more poultry availability while India has more pulses

FAOSTAT records 8.72 kg of poultry available per person per year in Pakistan and 3.52 in India in 2023. For pulses, the order reverses: 15.39 kg in India and 6.53 in Pakistan. Milk excluding butter is 87.43 kg in India and 129.71 in Pakistan. The source is the saved FAOSTAT food-balance release, with its flags and commodity definitions retained. [FAOSTAT food balances](<https://www.fao.org/faostat/en/#data/FBS>)

Food balance sheets estimate food available for human consumption within a national accounting system. They do not measure an individual's ingestion, household distribution or retail sales of alternative proteins. The series can compare recorded availability under the source's definitions. It cannot identify who consumed the food, how much was wasted after supply entered households or which products competed at a particular meal.

The pulse comparison matters because familiar plant foods are already part of ordinary food systems. Large pulse availability may be compatible with interest in a meat analogue, indifference to it or preference for a familiar dish. None of those responses follows from supply alone. A study that compares an analogue only with meat would miss a relevant existing option for some consumers.

The OECD–FAO Agricultural Outlook uses a separate method and weight convention. Its poultry projection is shown apart from the historical food-balance series, with its vintage and retail-weight unit. Joining the projected value directly to the FAOSTAT endpoint would create an apparent change partly from incompatible definitions. The report treats the outlook as a conditional projection from its source rather than an extension of measured household consumption. [OECD–FAO Agricultural Outlook](<https://sdmx.oecd.org/public/rest/data/OECD.TAD.ATM,DSD_AGR@DF_OUTLOOK_2025_2034,1.1/IND+PAK.A....?startPeriod=2010&amp;endPeriod=2034&amp;dimensionAtObservation=AllDimensions>)

For 2034, that outlook projects poultry consumption at 2.30 kg per person per year in India and 5.70 in Pakistan, in retail weight. The source release covers 2025–2034. The numerical difference from the food-balance endpoint is not an estimated decline under a common measurement system.

## Regional food frequency and household quantities answer different questions

India's National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5) measures reported food frequency among specified respondent groups. Its fish, chicken or meat category combines foods and records frequency rather than portion size. The regional panels retain the sex, age group, residence, fieldwork period and tabulation base applicable to each row. A weekly-frequency percentage cannot be converted to kilograms or grams of protein without information the table does not provide. [National Family Health Survey](<https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/FR375/FR375.pdf>)

Pakistan's Household Integrated Economic Survey, HIES, supplies household-based quantities and expenditure measures. The revised quantity workbook reports monthly per-capita quantities, so calling them household totals would change their meaning. Its regional chicken rows can describe differences within that survey's design. They cannot be placed on the same colour scale as India's reported weekly food frequency and interpreted as a bilateral dietary ranking. [HIES revised quantity table](<https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/TABLE_23-2.xls>)

The Pakistan National Nutrition Survey contributes separate nutrition context. Its population and indicators differ from HIES food quantities. The maps and tables retain geographic coverage gaps and distinguish the former merged areas where the source requires it. A missing join to the chosen boundary file is an unresolved mapping problem, not a zero observation.

Residence differences in these tables are descriptive. Urban and rural respondents or households differ in many measured and unmeasured circumstances. Neither the map nor the residence toggle identifies an effect of urbanisation. The useful follow-up is to examine access, prices, expenditure and meal practices in specified locations before selecting study sites.

## Food budgets and protein shares show different dietary structures

Pakistan's chicken quantity rises across the selected expenditure quintiles in the revised HIES table. This is a per-capita quantity gradient within one source, not a measured preference for an alternative product. Household size also differs across these groups, which makes denominator discipline necessary. A household total and a per-capita quantity can order groups differently without either describing the same estimand.

The lowest quintile records 0.17 kg of chicken per person per month, compared with 0.65 in the highest. The accompanying household-size context falls from 7.56 to 4.65 persons across those groups. The quantities come from the revised HIES 2024–25 workbook, with the precise cells recorded in the claims ledger. The exact analytical base for that revised table is not established by the report's broader household-count context.

India's Household Consumption Expenditure Survey, HCES, converts reported household food into nutrient estimates and reports protein contributions by food group. Cereals, pulses, dairy and the combined eggs, fish and meat group each have a distinct role. The combined animal-food group in that table excludes dairy. Its share cannot be described as all animal-source protein. [Nutritional Intake in India](<https://www.mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Nutritional_Intake_in_India_L.pdf>)

The Indian fractile labels rank the population by monthly per-capita consumption expenditure (MPCE), from lower to higher spending. Rural and urban populations are ranked separately. The labels describe positions in those expenditure distributions; they are not percentages below a protein requirement or shares of the food budget.

These tables broaden the comparative question beyond replacing meat. A product might compete with a cereal accompaniment, a pulse dish, dairy or a convenience meal. The report cannot determine that competition from ingredient composition. It requires asking consumers about a specified occasion and recording the food actually replaced.

Expenditure measures need equal care. A food-budget share is a proportion of food expenditure, not total household expenditure, energy or protein. A quantity gradient is not a price elasticity. The source rows retain those distinctions so that apparent agreement between indicators does not create an unsupported common scale.

## The Indian benchmark is a scenario and the Pakistan total is missing

India's published consumer-unit estimates are the appropriate denominator for the report's adult reference scenarios. A consumer unit expresses energy needs relative to a sedentary adult man. It is not a protein-equivalent person. Comparing a household group's mean protein estimate with an adult requirement therefore supplies a descriptive scenario, with an explicit mismatch that prevents an adequacy conclusion.

The all-India estimates are 66.6 g per consumer unit per day in rural areas and 69.9 in urban areas. HCES fieldwork ran from August 2023 through July 2024; these months describe the survey period, not an individual respondent's observation interval. The nutrient table's analytical base remains distinct from the survey's overall household sample.

The Estimated Average Requirement, EAR, is the primary reference; the Recommended Dietary Allowance, RDA, is retained as a labelled sensitivity. They answer different questions about requirements and cannot be swapped merely to produce a larger apparent gap. The report also retains alternative body-weight assumptions. None of these comparisons observes the distribution of usual individual intake needed for an estimate of population inadequacy.

At a reference weight of 60 kg, the EAR scenario uses 0.66 g per kg per day, or 39.60 g per day. The RDA sensitivity uses 0.83 g per kg per day, or 49.80 g per day. These calculations are tagged derived even though their requirement coefficients come from reference sources. They are adult scenarios without a protein-equivalent household denominator.

A group mean above a reference can coexist with individuals below it. A group mean below a reference does not tell how many individuals are below it. Counting expenditure groups is especially misleading when those groups differ in population or household composition. The former unweighted fraction of groups below a benchmark has therefore been removed rather than described as a population deficiency rate.

Pakistan's former full-diet estimate used a pooled-basket cost calibration that did not establish complete protein intake. The current model withdraws that total and preserves it only as historical output. The replacement converts selected reported food quantities through named food-composition factors and edible-portion assumptions. It reports a matched-food subtotal and a separate subtotal for chicken, beef, mutton, eggs and fish.

The composition evidence comes from the [Pakistan Food Composition Table](<https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/food_composition/documents/regional/Book_Food_Composition_Table_for_Pakistan_.pdf>), [Food Composition Table for Bangladesh](<https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/food_composition/documents/FCT_10_2_14_final_version.pdf>), [Indian Food Composition Tables](<https://www.nin.res.in/ebooks/IFCT2017.pdf>) and [USDA SR Legacy release](<https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/fdc-datasets/FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04.zip>). Pakistan-table food entries anchor the local matches; the Bangladesh Rohu entry supplies distinct edible-portion assumptions, while Indian and USDA entries supply explicitly named food-form alternatives. The [INFOODS density database](<https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/templates/food_composition/documents/density_DB_v2_0_01.pdf>) supports volume-to-mass assumptions. [USDA documentation](<https://www.ars.usda.gov/arsuserfiles/80400525/data/sr-legacy/sr-legacy_doc.pdf>) defines the edible-portion basis; its portion weights do not establish Pakistani egg-size frequencies. The model also treats tea-transfer and product-mixture choices as conditional assumptions. These sources provide composition and method inputs, not measured Pakistani intake.

For each named endpoint, the [scenario-factor table](../methods/factor_evidence.csv) identifies the conditional food conversions. Its factor identifiers join the [source-factor catalogue](../methods/source_factor_catalog.csv), which gives original PDF pages, food codes, units and source-file hashes. The claims ledger preserves that join and the complete composition-source list alongside the household quantity cells. The [method guide](../methods/README.md) explains how to follow this record without treating an assumed food match as an observed product mix.

The national matched-food subtotal is 39.27 to 40.61 g per person per day. The separate chicken, beef, mutton, eggs and fish subtotal is 3.30 to 4.64 g per person per day. The low method is `bd_cuts_rohu_us_small`; the high method is `edible_flesh_us_jumbo`. Their differences concern edible portions, fish matching and egg-size assumptions, rather than sampled uncertainty.

Of 38 food rows, 27 receive a conditional conversion; 0 are imputed. The separate animal-food subtotal excludes dairy and other animal-source foods. Neither subtotal is called total protein intake, and no quintile ordering is inferred from these incomplete sums.

The method range expresses the extrema among named conditional conversions. It is not a sampling confidence interval or a complete uncertainty bound. Unresolved foods, prepared-food coverage, food-form mixtures and quantity reconciliation remain outside the subtotal. No imputation is used to silently complete the diet. The range therefore remains separate from the Indian consumer-unit panel and from the adult EAR/RDA scenarios.

The unresolved food quantities and local product mixtures are the next inputs needed before a complete Pakistani intake estimate can be attempted.
