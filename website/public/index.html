India and Pakistan: what spending scenarios can establish

Local review candidate, 27 October 2026. AI-assisted; human factual/code verification and joint-report acceptance pending.

The question and the evidence

How might spending on alternative-protein foods develop in India and Pakistan, and what would make those paths more credible? The available evidence gives us several partial answers. Food accounts describe supply, surveys describe stated intentions, and business directories describe activity. They do not provide a national record of repeated purchases of meat, dairy and egg alternatives. We therefore construct conditional spending scenarios and identify the observations that would test their assumptions. Spending is the outcome here; it does not by itself measure adoption, protein intake or displacement of animal foods.

Drivers and obstacles come first

Demand depends on a product reaching a buyer, fitting a meal and budget, and being bought again. The eight factors below organize the retained evidence. Their effects may differ across products, income groups and places. The fuller driver tables and evidence ledger distinguish India evidence from Pakistan evidence, upward pressures from obstacles, and measured facts from hypotheses. The three US themes raised in the review - politics, health and R&D - are comparison prompts pending verification of the original study, rather than findings imposed on South Asia.1

The evidence does not support treating these as eight independently estimated effects. For example, Bryant and colleagues’ Indian online sample was comparatively urban, affluent and educated and measured hypothetical willingness, not later purchases. GFI India’s talent study reports 20 industry responses and 19 academic participants, not a national workforce census. Policy announcements and institutional powers likewise need to be distinguished from implementation and product-specific approval.234

What we did

We began with GFI India/Deloitte’s estimate of India’s 2022 smart-protein market: INR 351 crore across plant-based meat, dairy and eggs. Its source does not specify the price base or establish retail valuation. Treating it as nominal 2022 retail spending, inflating it and converting at the 2025 exchange rate gives about $45.3 million. This is a converted external estimate, not an observed 2025 market size.5

We carried India’s spending per person forward using real income, urbanisation and an additional annual growth term. The latter uses selected sales and vacancy proxies for conditions not identified separately. We then transferred that path to Pakistan using relative income, urban share, animal-protein supply and industry activity, and multiplied by each country’s population. These relationships were chosen, not fitted to a series of alternative-protein purchases. Four parameters were drawn 10,000 times, with each draw reused across countries and years. A plain-language walkthrough and exact equations make each step inspectable.6

Why the scenarios sit below GFI India

The gap is partly mechanical: our chosen bounds do not permit GFI’s low target under the retained price conversion. India’s 2030 median is $187.8 million; changing the US-derived additional-growth floor from -2% to 0% raises it to $202.2 million, while even the theoretical maximum under the original bounds is $462.0 million. GFI’s low case converts conditionally to $964.4 million. GFI describes socioeconomic-group adoption, substitution and paths to taste/price parity supported by localization, R&D and talent investment. Our aggregate equation cannot identify those mechanisms separately. Whether GFI’s faster expansion or our bounds are more defensible remains an empirical question; the discrepancy itself does not validate either approach.8

US benchmark and the separate elicitation

The 2035 middle scenarios correspond to about $0.28 per person in India and $0.040 in Pakistan: roughly 1.22% and 0.17% of the fixed US 2025 plant-based retail benchmark. These are per-person comparisons with a broader product basket, not shares of the US total market or comparisons with a US 2035 forecast. US data also inform the model’s elasticity ceiling and growth floor; the new diagnostics vary those roles separately.1011

The separate 60-response elicitation describes repeated AI-generated projections under recorded workflows. Its regional headline excludes fermentation, whereas the proxy includes it. Its spread describes run medians, not calibrated sales uncertainty. GFI supplied our proxy baseline and appeared with forward scenarios in the regional evidence packets, so agreement among these strands would not establish independent corroboration. Umar’s supplementary US arm remains separate from the original US panel and from any claim of joint-author approval.12

What follows from the evidence

The contribution is an explicit account of which assumptions generate the country estimates and which observations would challenge them. Recommendations therefore take the form of bounded tests, with no claimed ranking of investment returns. The recommendation register specifies proposed actors, comparators, outcomes and limits; the following IDs connect each proposal to the drivers above.13

  • R01 - comparable retail prices, availability and purchases (D01, D02, D07). Record defined portions, prices and stock conditions for matched products and outlets, then relate them to purchases rather than infer demand from listings.
  • R02 - meal fit and paid repeat purchase (D02, D03, D04). Test a specified product against a familiar meal alternative and distinguish trial, unsubsidized repeat purchase and any animal-food displacement.
  • R03 - product-specific fermentation assurance (D05, D06). Assemble the ingredient/process dossier needed for qualified certification and separate food-authorization clarification. General halal institutions do not settle a particular product’s status.
  • R04 and R05 - bounded technical work (D07). In India, test an employer-nominated bottleneck against baseline performance. In Pakistan, first assess whether the facilities, staff and ingredient-application task support a feasible pilot. Sampled talent concerns do not establish nationwide shortages.
  • R06 - AI/non-AI technical comparison (D07, D08). Compare validated output, cost and performance against a credible non-AI baseline and examine whether any gain reaches retail prices or availability.14
  • R07 - dated policy implementation register (D05, D06). Track the move from announcements to documented implementation and uptake. Keep absent evidence unresolved rather than treating it as no activity.

Methods appendix: India–Pakistan spending scenarios

AI-assisted review draft, 27 October 2026. Human factual/code verification pending. Proposed joint-report methods contribution; definitions are proposed harmonization, not a recorded team agreement.

Read this first: the calculation in plain language

We ask how domestic spending on alternative-protein foods might develop in India and Pakistan in 2026, 2030 and 2035. We do not measure national adoption or predict how much conventional meat will be replaced. The following steps explain the retained model; the exact equations below are unchanged.

  1. Start with India. GFI India/Deloitte estimates a 2022 market of INR 351 crore for plant-based meat, dairy and eggs. We assume a 2022 price basis and retail valuation, then convert it to 2025-price dollars: about $45.3 million. These interpretations are assumptions because the source does not specify them.
  2. Carry spending per person forward. Income growth and urbanisation raise or lower the path. An additional annual growth term represents other changes imperfectly; its bounds come from a US sales decline and Indian vacancy growth. These are chosen proxies, not relationships fitted to purchases.
  3. Construct Pakistan’s conditional path. There is no equivalent verified national sales anchor in the retained evidence. We transfer India’s per-person path using relative income, urban share, animal-protein supply and industry-activity proxies, then multiply by Pakistan’s population. This adds uncertainty and does not establish observed Pakistani sales.
  4. Repeat under different assumptions. The model draws four parameters 10,000 times. Each draw is reused across countries and years. The lower, middle and upper values are the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of those calculations; they are not calibrated probabilities of future sales.
  5. Change assumptions deliberately. Retained sensitivities test overlapping income/industry growth and Pakistan multipliers. The new GFI companion also changes the US-derived growth floor and elasticity assumptions separately and calculates the extra growth required to reach GFI’s targets. It leaves the historical reference results unchanged.
  6. Interpret the limits. GFI’s domestic scenarios, our assumption percentiles and repeated AI responses are different objects. Shared GFI inputs prevent treating their agreement as independent confirmation. The main empirical need is comparable sales and repeat-purchase evidence, especially for Pakistan.

For the numerical comparison, see Why these scenarios sit below GFI India. Drivers and proposed tests explain the mechanisms that the aggregate equation cannot identify separately.

1. Question, estimand and evidence classes

The calculation explores domestic alternative-protein spending in India and Pakistan in 2026, 2030 and 2035. Its intended scope is plant-based replacement foods and fermentation-derived human foods, excluding cultivated meat. The 2022 Indian anchor contains plant-based meat, dairy and eggs; the source’s forward domestic categories include fermentation-derived meat. The model carries one aggregate forward and does not separately estimate entry of every proposed product category. Its interpretation must retain that limitation.

The proposed valuation is retail spending in market-exchange-rate US dollars at constant 2025 prices. Retail valuation and the Indian source’s nominal-2022 price basis are assumptions. They are not explicit properties established by the original GFI/Deloitte summary. US coverage is broader and includes prepared meals. These differences prevent a fully matched cross-country market comparison.

Observed macro quantities, published external market estimates, analyst scenarios, model-elicited projections and missing evidence are recorded as different classes. The proxy scenarios estimate neither adoption probabilities nor physical protein volumes. FAOSTAT supply is an input proxy, not household consumption or alternative-protein demand. No model output is converted into an observed baseline.

The model was not fitted to a time series of alternative-protein purchases, and its future outcomes are unresolved. Calibration, predictive accuracy and probability coverage have not been demonstrated. Country-specific inference is limited to India and Pakistan.

3. Exact implemented transformations

Notation: country c is India I or Pakistan P; year is t; P_c(t) denotes population in millions, U_c(t) urban share in percent, I_c(t) real GDP per person relative to 2022, s_c(t) annual dollars per person, and M_c(t) total spending in million dollars. Percentage units cancel in urban-share ratios.

3.1 Price conversion and anchors

Let F = (1 + 5.4/100)(1 + 4.6/100)(1 + 2.0/100), using Indian consumer-price inflation in 2023–2025. Let X = 87.158448 INR per USD, the retained rounded WDI 2025 period-average exchange rate.

Indian anchor A = 351 × 10 × F / X = 45.286640 million USD at 2025 prices
s_I(2022) = A / P_I(2022) = 0.031771 USD per person
s_US(2025) = 7.9 × 1,000 / P_US(2025) = 23.106847 USD per person

The factor 10 converts crore rupees to million rupees. The US 2025 dollars already use the selected price year. The Indian anchor is a 2022 observation-year estimate converted to 2025 prices, not a 2025 market observation.

3.2 Population and real-income paths

The retained IMF population series is used through 2031. Beyond it:

P_c(t) = P_c(2031) × [P_c(2031)/P_c(2030)]^(t−2031), for t > 2031
I_c(2022) = 1
I_c(t) = product over y=2023…t of [(1 + g_c(min(y,2031))/100)
                                      / (P_c(y)/P_c(y−1))]

Here g is real GDP growth. Holding the 2031 GDP and population growth rates through 2035 is an analyst extrapolation, not an additional IMF forecast. Retained 2035 income indices are 2.167826 for India and 1.254629 for Pakistan.

For each country, ordinary least squares on the eleven annual urban-share observations from 2015 through 2025 supplies a slope b_c. Future urban share is U_c(2025) + b_c(t−2025). The function uses observed values through 2025. No estimated uncertainty or cross-country harmonization is added. Retained 2035 shares are 38.831347% and 43.289498%.

3.3 Parameter bounds

Four parameters are independently sampled from uniform distributions once per draw. The same sampled parameters are reused across years and countries within that draw; country/year results are therefore related, not independent samples.

The full-precision values are computed by the retained code from inputs, not from the rounded bounds above. Directory counts are 119 for India and 2 for Pakistan. 2025 vacancies are 529 and 58. Neither defines a complete national industry census.

For the upper elasticity bound, India’s 2022 GDP per person in local currency is inflated by F and divided by X. US 2025 GDP per person is already in market dollars at 2025 prices. Then:

e_max = log[s_US(2025)/s_I(2022)]
        / log[GDPpc_US(2025)/(GDPpc_I(2022, INR) × F/X)]

The retained spending and income ratios are 727.301293 and 38.081810. They have a common price basis but different observation years. A same-price-basis conversion does not make them a matched contemporaneous comparison. Urbanisation, tastes, availability, category coverage and measurement also contribute to the observed gap; attributing it entirely to income is only a bounding assumption.

3.4 India and Pakistan equations

s_I(t) = s_I(2022) × I_I(t)^e × [U_I(t)/U_I(2022)] × (1+d)^(t−2022)

R_income(t) = [GDPpc_P(2025)/GDPpc_I(2025)]
              × [I_P(t)/I_P(2025)] / [I_I(t)/I_I(2025)]

s_P(t) = s_I(t) × R_income(t)^e × [U_P(t)/U_I(t)] × R_animal^w × k

M_c(t) = s_c(t) × P_c(t)
US comparison percentage_c(t) = 100 × s_c(t)/s_US(2025)

The 2025 Pakistan/India income ratio is 0.590535. R_animal = 31.52/19.67 = 1.602440…, from the sum of bovine meat, eggs, fish/seafood, milk excluding butter, mutton/goat, pigmeat and poultry protein supply. The industry and dietary multipliers are not independently identified effects. Their multiplication may double-count correlated conditions.

No Pakistan retail observation is used to fit or validate the transfer equation. Missing baseline evidence is never assigned a zero. No separate AI or policy multiplier is applied. No cultivated-meat entry term is present.

4. Draws, aggregation and output

The implementation uses Python’s standard-library pseudorandom generator, random.Random(20261015), and 10,000 draws. Every iteration draws e, d, w, k in that order. Results for each country/year are sorted. For quantile fraction q, compute h=(n−1)q, i=floor(h), j=ceil(h), and return x[i]+(x[j]−x[i])(h−i) with zero-based indexing. Output CSV values are formatted to six decimal places.

The main table reports q=.1, .5 and .9. They describe variation under chosen parameter distributions. They are not confidence bounds, a calibrated 80% predictive interval, or empirical household uncertainty. Display rounding must not imply greater accuracy. The chapter uses $448m and $11.8m for the retained 2035 medians 447.504341 and 11.759263.

5. Retained sensitivities and external benchmark

Sensitivities reset the same seed, retaining paired draws. Even a fixed or unused parameter is drawn before being replaced; this preserves pairing.

  1. Income/drift overlap: annual real income growth from 2023 to 2025 is 6.548202%. The alternative upper drift is d_max − income_growth × e_max = 5.977774%; the lower bound stays −2%. India’s 2035 p10/p50/p90 become 134.658168 / 215.060659 / 341.253412 million; Pakistan’s become 2.382182 / 5.964900 / 11.690860 million. This is a sensitivity, not a fitted correction.
  2. Pakistan overlap: set its relative urban share and animal-supply multipliers to 1 while retaining India’s path, income ratio and industry factor. Pakistan’s 2035 p10/p50/p90 become 2.575709 / 8.318219 / 25.341349 million.
  3. One parameter fixed: hold each parameter at its lower and upper bound while drawing the others. India’s 2035 median ranges are 332.438965–622.332639 million for elasticity and 127.357046–1398.084812 for drift. Pakistan’s corresponding pairs are 12.293369 / 11.255477 for elasticity, 3.652403 / 40.094912 for drift, 9.273218 / 14.859778 for animal weight, and 3.468582 / 22.627744 for industry factor. These are pairs of conditional medians, not uncertainty intervals or variance decompositions. Higher elasticity need not increase Pakistan’s transfer estimate because its relative income factor is below one.

The existing comparison converts GFI India’s 2030 external scenarios using the same 2022-to-2025 inflation and exchange-rate transformation as the anchor, yielding 964.437694 / 1648.769136 / 2446.639731 million. This assumes the external scenarios share that source price base; the summary does not establish it. The fact that all retained proxy draws fall below GFI’s low case is a conditional disagreement, not a validation test or proof of which forecast is correct. Preserve source rupee values alongside any converted comparison until the price basis is resolved.

6. Behaviour, policy and talent evidence

Behavioural evidence is read for the actual question and sampled population. Stated willingness, paid first purchase, unsubsidized repeat purchase and animal-food displacement are separate outcomes. The selective nine-study extraction package has no India finding and is not a systematic review of national adoption; the bilateral discussion draws on additional studies. Do not turn “not measured in these sources” into “does not occur.”

Policy sources establish the dated instrument or announcement and its stated jurisdiction. Implementation, disbursement, product authorization and uptake need different evidence. Halal status and food authorization are distinct questions. This appendix does not provide a legal determination or assume a product’s certification from general institutional powers.

Talent evidence separates job-board advertisements, directory entries, sampled employer testimony and national workforce measures. GFI India’s study’s 20 industry responses and 19 academic participants are study cohorts. Its findings may motivate a pilot; they do not identify a nationally optimal training portfolio. Recommendations in the chapter are author proposals that require baseline and outcome checks.

Which model factors produce the Indian path?

For any one draw, the spending multiple is exactly:

M_India(2030) / A = population_ratio × income_index^elasticity
                   × urban_share_ratio × (1 + extra_growth)^8

Here A is the converted 2022 anchor, not a 2025 observation. The following illustration fixes elasticity and extra growth at their parameter midpoints. It is not the median of the simulated totals.

The target T and anchor A must share the stated price basis. The figure varies income elasticity across the retained bounds. The dot uses midpoint elasticity; the range is assumption sensitivity, not a predictive interval.

Testing the US-derived assumptions separately

US evidence sets an upper elasticity bound and a lower extra-growth bound, as well as providing a descriptive 2025 spending benchmark. A change to the growth floor alone therefore does not remove US influence. The companion consumes the same four uniform draws in the same order for every case; it rescales the growth draw into a new interval rather than clipping negative values to zero.

Four questions raised by the comparison

Are older data responsible? The GFI summary uses a 2022 baseline/context, but its exact publication and model-development dates are unresolved. Dated 2021-22 US releases contain both upbeat sector messaging and evidence of slowing growth. They establish context, not why Deloitte selected particular assumptions. The bounded dated-source review therefore leaves the causal explanation untested.

Does GFI include factors our model lacks? Its methodology explicitly discusses socioeconomic-group penetration, adoption, replacement, relative conventional/plant-based prices, taste and price parity, supply-chain localization, R&D and talent investment. Page 2 attributes scenario construction to GFI’s target-market research and Deloitte’s proprietary market-sizing, with expert interviews informing taste/price parity. Our aggregate model has no separately identified price-parity or category-entry term. Residual growth may implicitly reflect some of these mechanisms; their individual contributions cannot be recovered from the equation. This is a documented difference in representation, not an additive attribution of the numerical gap.16

Is projection from the US inappropriate? Our starting market estimate is Indian, while two parameter bounds use US evidence. The paired tests above show their numerical influence separately. The original US panel’s politics/health/R&D themes remain reviewer-supplied prompts for the country-specific driver comparison, pending verified provenance of that study. A US trend cannot establish the same mechanism or effect size in India or Pakistan.

What else could matter? Source price/retail bases, observation years, product/channel scope, scenario definitions, chosen parameter distributions and proxy validity remain material. GFI supplies our baseline and appeared with forward estimates in the regional elicitation packets. Neither matching GFI nor agreement with those responses is independent validation. GFI’s public summary does not disclose sufficient detailed proprietary parameters to calculate a definitive causal decomposition.

6. Aggregation and retained results

For each model, region, measure and year, the summary takes each accepted response’s stated P50 and computes empirical percentiles across those run medians. It does not pool all response P10/P50/P90 values into one predictive distribution. Original quantiles are retained in the numeric response archive. Missing conditional values are excluded for that measure and n_nonmissing is reported; missingness is not filled with zero.

Sort the run medians as x[0]…x[n−1]. For fraction q, let h=(n−1)q, i=floor(h), j=min(i+1,n−1); return x[i]+(x[j]−x[i])(h−i). For ten nonmissing runs, the median is the mean of the fifth and sixth ordered values, P10 is 0.1×first + 0.9×second, and P90 is 0.9×ninth + 0.1×tenth.

For a purely illustrative ordered set of ten run medians 1,2,…,10, this rule gives P10=1.9, P50=5.5 and P90=9.1. These example numbers explain the interpolation; they are not study results or a model’s own predictive quantiles.

Main-text limitations

The India–Pakistan results are conditional spending scenarios. The Indian anchor is an external modeled estimate with an unstated price and valuation basis; the analysis assumes nominal 2022 rupees and retail value. Pakistan lacks a comparable verified national baseline in the retained evidence. The separate model-elicitation supplement is a repetition and disagreement exercise under recorded workflows. Similar answers do not prove calibration or independent evidence; different answers do not isolate the effect of a model, prompt or AI assumption. The supplement is authored as Umar’s work and is not represented as an accepted joint result.

The GFI comparison is an inverse-model diagnostic under an unresolved source price basis. Original rupee values accompany conditional dollar conversions.
