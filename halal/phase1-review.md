# Cultivated Chicken Religious Conditions and Market Access

Phase One research review by Muhammad Umar Zafar · 30 September 2026

AI-assisted working draft. The author recorded human factual and code verification on 10 November 2026. Public-source repository edition; not mentor-approved and not reviewed by a qualified scholar or lawyer. See the evidence registers and release scope.

The sources support a conditional, process-specific account of cultivated chicken. Donor origin, procurement, culture inputs and the identity of the resulting material remain decisive questions. Contemporary institutional statements create routes for further assessment, but the reviewed sources do not establish one rule shared by every school, a halal certificate for a named product, or permission to sell it in every focal country.

This review executes the revised local plan whose milestones were moved to 30 September. This public edition covers nine questions across five traditions, institutional positions, seven country cases and four historical comparisons. India, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates are the focal countries. Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia supply precedents. The Jafari column is deliberately limited to Ali al-Sistani’s named positions.

## Main findings

The apparent simplicity of a no-slaughter production stage conceals a separate donor question. Ghamidi’s published discussion treats flesh detached from a living animal as maytah. This supplies counter-evidence to an unrestricted no-killing argument without itself classifying cultured founder cells. [GHAMIDI; AD2858]

Transformation is a disputed legal test, not a synonym for processing. The selected Hanafi discussion allows consequential changes while preserving dissent; Shafii and the familiar Hanbali accounts are more restrictive, with exceptions and alternatives. Maliki texts require distinctions between complete change and disputed cases. Sistani requires a change of essence and retains impurity when that change is uncertain. [HAN; SHA-T; H-MUGH-IST; H-TAY-IST; M-DHAK; SIS-EN]

Regulatory evidence is strongest for specified Singapore processes, including separate serum-containing and serum-free chicken entries. Saudi materials describe a novel-food route that expressly includes animal cell culture. India provides a general non-specified-food route. The UAE announcement examined describes development of a framework, while Pakistan’s halal statute alone does not resolve cultivated-food authorization. [SG-LIST; SA-NOVEL; IN2017; AE-2025; PK2016]

## Terms used throughout

Maytah denotes a legal carcass category; biological cell viability does not itself decide that category. Istihalah is a legally consequential transformation; its required degree varies by authority. Istihlak concerns disappearance or absorption within another substance and must not be conflated with transformation. Purity, permission to eat, certification and civil market authorization are recorded separately. [HAN-D; SIS-EN; IIFA198; IIFA210]

## Published dietary context

Ghamidi’s authored essay, translated by Shehzad Saleem, connects dietary categories with human nature and discusses the four prohibitions. It also includes flesh removed from a living animal within maytah. Its invocation discussion quotes Amin Ahsan Islahi and links the act to the sanctity of life. These published passages introduce a donor constraint that a purely no-killing argument would miss. They do not expressly classify cultured founder cells. [GHAMIDI]

The Quranic passages 7:157, 6:145, 5:3 and 6:121 and the detached-part report in Abu Dawud anchor distinct questions. Their relation to a modern process must be argued, not assumed. The four-prohibitions formulation cannot be used as a complete description of every later school manual: the inspected manuals and Sistani’s rulings contain further operative restrictions. [Q7; Q6; Q5; Q6121; AD2858; H4; S2; J6]

## Institutional positions and changing precedents

## IIFA Resolution 265

Resolution 265 retains living-donor and lawfully slaughtered-donor alternatives in its English and Arabic texts. The living-donor wording should not be silently restricted to an unstated species list. Other conditions concern permitted inputs, specialist supervision, disclosure and safety. The resolution’s conditional permission to consume and market is an institutional religious position, not a national food license or a named product certificate. Its council session was 4–8 May 2025; the web publication is dated 20 November 2025. [IIFA265; IIFA-AR]

The clause matters because it complicates any proposed universal prerequisite of prior donor slaughter. It does not establish that every live biopsy meets the resolution, resolve the legal status of each founder line or demonstrate agreement with every classical school account. Those are separate interpretive questions. Comparing the Arabic and English versions checks wording while remaining within one institutional source family.

## MUIS and the fuller certification discussion

The MUIS release of 3 February 2024 lists permitted animal sources, halal ingredients and a clean, non-toxic product. Silence about slaughter in a short release is not an exemption. The fuller 2024 monograph, From Lab to Table, describes slaughtered permitted animals for the then-current Singapore certification framework on printed page 14, and separately discusses egg embryos without slaughter. Page 19 says certification guidelines still need development. Their precise relationship, and their current operational application, require a current certificate or scheme document. [MUIS2024; MUIS-BOOK]

The comparison therefore retains a tension rather than resolving it by omission: IIFA’s donor wording and MUIS’s certification discussion are not identical documents performing identical functions. A broad religious condition, a certifier’s operational requirement and a food regulator’s safety decision can legitimately address different objects. The source record must identify which object is being discussed.

## Why later texts change an analogy

IIFA Resolution 198 in 2013 associated transmutation with real change and treated plasma differently from whole blood. Resolution 210 in 2015 retained the transformation definition but reopened the plasma issue because of new information, and left gelatin for further study. Its rennet discussion records disagreement on unslaughtered sources and permits a genetic-engineering route. The 2015 page is an IIFA resolution that quotes earlier IOMS material; it should not be mislabeled merely as the 1995 seminar. [IIFA198; IIFA210]

This sequence weakens an argument that cites the earlier plasma paragraph as a settled institutional authorization for culture serum. Plasma, serum, removal and transformation need their own analysis. The history also shows why a citation’s date and subsequent reconsideration belong inside the claim record.

## What the school comparison establishes

The common research problem is classification. The same process can raise a donor question, a contamination question and a transformation question at different stages. The appendix provides nine bounded answers for each tradition, including the evidence that limits each answer. The profiles below explain where the disagreements alter the next research step.

## Food categories and invocation

Accounts of tayyib and khabith do not reduce to a consumer’s individual disgust. Al-Kasani refers to sound natures; Nawawi and Ibn Qudama discuss qualified customary judgments, with textual rules and analogy constraining their application. Al-Qurtubi attributes to Malik a reading tied to legal permission and prohibition. The exact Sistani criterion sought here remains open. These differences matter to claims that unfamiliarity itself makes cultivated food objectionable. [H3; S1; H-MUGH-FOOD; M-QUR7157]

The selected Hanafi, Maliki and Hanbali slaughter texts distinguish intentional omission of invocation from forgetting. Nawawi’s preferred Shafii account treats invocation as recommended and does not invalidate slaughter through omission, while preserving another internal view on deliberate omission. Sistani requires invocation by the slaughterer and expressly excuses forgetting. Dedication to another deity remains a different question from simply omitting words. [H4; M-RIS29; H-UM-SLAUGHTER; S7; J5]

## Detached material and transformation

The inspected authors classify flesh detached during legally relevant life as carcass or impure material, with species and substance exceptions. Some distinguish removal after effective slaughter from removal while stable life remains. The inference for cultivation must identify whether it concerns the original biopsy, isolated cells, a maintained line or harvested descendants. None of those objects can simply inherit a completed ruling from a sentence about gross tissue without an argument. [HAN-D; SHA-D; M-DAS30; H-MUGH-PARTS; J2]

Ibn Abidin preserves a selected transformative-purification position and disagreement. Nawawi rejects a general ash-or-salt route in his preferred account while discussing alternatives and the wine exception. Ibn Qudama’s restrictive account and Ibn Taymiyya’s broader preference cannot both be labeled unanimous Hanbali doctrine. Al-Qarafi records disagreement and distinguishes complete loss of impurity-defining qualities from lesser change. Sistani’s requirement of changed essence likewise makes a process description indispensable. [HAN; SHA-T; H-MUGH-IST; H-TAY-IST; M-DHAK; SIS-EN]

## Exceptions cannot be transferred between traditions

Liver and spleen are distinguished from flowing blood in the cited Sunni passages. Sistani prohibits spleen among land-animal parts; the corresponding bird-organ restriction is obligatory precaution. A direct affirmative liver ruling was not extracted. Aquatic rules also vary: fish-only formulations, broader marine permissions, species exclusions and capture conditions require separate statements. Neither purity nor absence of land-animal slaughter establishes edibility across these accounts. [H7; S6; H-MUGH-LIVER; J6; H4; M-MUW25; H-UM-FOOD2; J7]

## Focal country requirements

The comparison records what the retrieved instrument establishes, its institutional level and the remaining product question. These are research findings from dated sources, not a determination that a manufacturer can lawfully launch. A product-specific decision must match the process, applicant and jurisdiction.

## India

FSSAI’s 2017 regulations, read through the 2022 compendium, require prior approval before manufacture or import of non-specified foods or ingredients. Form I includes novel foods and technologies and requests origin, composition, process and safety information. The regulations distinguish approval from the subsequent licensing application. This provides a general route; it does not document a cultivated-chicken approval. The current landing page returned a JavaScript shell, so the complete latest application list was not audited. [IN2017]

## Pakistan

The Pakistan Halal Authority Act 2016 establishes certification, standards, accreditation and logo functions. Its nationwide scope covers imports, exports and international or interprovincial halal trade; other statutory purposes apply to Islamabad Capital Territory. Sections 17–20 have deferred commencement provisions. These details prevent treating the Act as a complete nationwide cultivated-food approval rule. The appropriate provincial food authority, operative notifications and a product classification remain unresolved. [PK2016]

## Saudi Arabia

The SFDA novel-food text expressly includes food produced from animal cell or tissue culture, requires official approval before marketing and refers to halal sources and the GSO halal standard. The guide asks for process, composition, safety and exposure information. A December 2025 announcement confirms a dedicated route. The official English documents display inconsistent regulation numbers: 513/2020, 5031:2020 and 5013. The route is documented, but the controlling version and number should be confirmed from authoritative Arabic before a product application. No named chicken authorization is established here. [SA-NOVEL; SA-GUIDE; SA-2025]

## United Arab Emirates

MOIAT describes certification through registered bodies within the UAE halal system and separately calls the Halal National Mark optional. That distinction does not mean every certificate is optional, nor that every food falls within an identical obligation. WAM’s 24 October 2025 headline announces a novel-food framework, but the article body describes an initiative to develop it. This source cannot establish a completed national route or a product approval. The operative ADAFSA instrument and the relevant federal or emirate registration requirements remain the necessary next documents. [AE-HALAL; AE-2025]

## Comparison countries and documented processes

## Singapore

SFA’s current framework requires premarket approval for covered novel foods. Its page updated 27 August 2026 describes the FSSA framework, scientific safety evidence and possible cancellation when relevant conditions or process information change. The 14 August 2026 approved list distinguishes Eat Just’s serum-containing chicken process allowed 26 November 2020, its serum-free process allowed 11 January 2023, and Vital Meat’s embryonic stem-cell biomass allowed 17 October 2025. The entries have different input histories and should not be merged into a generic approved chicken process. [SG-FRAME; SG-LIST]

The Vital Meat entry describes production medium without animal-derived components while identifying chicken embryonic stem cells as the production strain. That is a concrete reason to separate animal-free medium from an animal-free product. The approval list supplies technical and regulatory evidence. It supplies no matching halal certificate in this review. The MUIS position must therefore remain a separate layer. [SG-LIST; MUIS2024; MUIS-BOOK]

## Malaysia

JAKIM’s English portal explains foreign-body recognition and the conditions for describing imported goods as halal. It also describes meat-import certification and plant approval, while still citing an older standards edition. The portal supports an institutional map, not a current cultivated-meat-specific authorization. Specific Malaysian cultivated-meat leads found in Malay remain held outside the accepted English-first, Arabic-fallback lane until a suitable primary version is obtained. [MY-HALAL]

## Indonesia

The official English translation of BPJPH Decree 221/2025 provides a foreign-certificate registration process. It was issued 15 September 2025 and takes effect three months later, replacing Decree 90/2023. The procedure requires recognized foreign certificates to be registered before circulation and identifies the importer or local representative as applicant through SIHALAL. Indonesian prevails if the translations differ. This is evidence about halal administration; it does not establish food-safety approval or a cultivated-chicken product registration. [ID221]

## Historical parallels tested against production

An analogy is useful when it identifies which property must be established and what would defeat the comparison. It becomes misleading when a familiar precedent replaces a description of the new process. The following four comparisons remain analytical inferences; their premises are separately sourced.

## Wine becoming vinegar

The transformation texts direct attention to changed identity and properties, with important differences among authorities. A cultivated process involves multiplication of identified animal cells; this description alone does not demonstrate that the material has become a legally different substance. The comparison therefore generates a test: identify each disputed input and show what it becomes, rather than treating growth or a changed commercial name as proof of transformation. A named authority’s criterion must govern the assessment. [HAN; SHA-T; SIS-EN; IIFA198; SG-LIST]

## Rennet and manufactured enzymes

Rennet precedents show why the route of production can matter even when the intended function is similar. IIFA Resolution 210 differentiates extracted sources and records disagreement, while allowing genetically engineered rennet. The Hanbali and Hanafi materials also retain rennet-specific disagreements. An enzyme supplier’s source, production host, substrate and processing aids are thus relevant evidence. The analogy ends where rennet is only an ingredient while cultivated cells are the intended biomass. Permission for one input does not settle the founder line. [IIFA210; H-MUGH-RENNET; H6]

## Blood serum and removal

The GOOD Meat dossier hosted by FDA describes serum use and quantifies residual bovine serum albumin after processing. It is the applicant’s primary process account, not independent replication or a halal judgment. Washing can lower carryover without showing zero residue or the kind of identity change required by a particular jurist. The separate SFA serum-free entry also prevents treating the older serum-containing dossier as the description of every later process. [GOOD-DOSSIER; SG-LIST]

The analogy is further limited by IIFA’s reopening of the plasma issue in 2015. A blood-derived fraction cannot be assumed to have the same legal treatment as whole blood, another fraction or a wholly transformed substance. Process evidence must distinguish removal, dilution, assimilation and transformation; the research does not decide their legal equivalence. [IIFA198; IIFA210]

## Detached flesh and egg embryos

The detached-part precedent bears most directly on procurement. MUIS’s egg discussion and IIFA’s living-donor clause complicate attempts to universalize one procurement rule. A real comparison should follow the sample through founder-cell isolation, banking, serial growth and harvest. The question is whether, and at which stage, the chosen authority treats the resulting material as the original detached part or another legal object. Cell viability alone answers neither side. [AD2858; HAN-D; SHA-D; MUIS-BOOK; IIFA265]

## Counter evidence and Phase Two handoff

## Claims that did not survive the review

The source set does not support an unrestricted no-killing argument, a universal prior-slaughter requirement, a claim that processing automatically purifies, or an inference that a food approval certifies halal status. It also does not support treating an institution’s translated pages as independent corroboration or treating every reported consensus as universal agreement. These rejected shortcuts identify where the next analysis must become more specific.

The strongest competing interpretation is that cultivated chicken remains sufficiently continuous with the donor and its inputs for inherited restrictions to matter. A rival interpretation emphasizes legally meaningful changes or authorized modern source routes. The current evidence establishes the terms of that disagreement. It cannot choose between them for a real product without its process history and a named authority’s assessment.

## The next unit of research

Phase Two should select one documented chicken process and build a dossier that can be assessed against more than one authority. The SFA entries offer concrete candidates, but a public summary is not a complete dossier. Begin with the founder line: species, tissue, embryonic or adult origin, procurement timing and any slaughter documentation. Then trace cell banking and every culture stage, including historical serum use, enzymes, growth factors, scaffold and processing aids.

For each disputed input, record the material introduced, its fate, removal steps, residual assay and detection limit. Establish the final composition and match the technical account to the exact food approval. Only then compare the donor, impurity, transformation and edibility tests. Record the certifying body, certificate scope, validity and facility separately from the food authority’s decision. A current certificate must be checked directly before any certification claim is made.

## Hanafi source account

Nine bounded answers from the named sources. Modern applications remain inferences. Full qualifications and review decisions are retained in the school register.

1 Food categories and authority. Al-Kasani uses the aversion of sound natures to explain the prohibition of certain repulsive animals, with locust as a textual exception. [H3]

2 Scope of the four prohibitions. Al-Quduri also prohibits predatory animals and domestic donkeys. Thus the four-item Qur’anic formulation is not the manual’s complete operative dietary list. [H4]

3 Carcasses and detached parts. Ibn Abidin treats a part severed from a living animal as its carcass. A part removed after valid slaughter, while residual life remains, is distinguished. [H2]

4 Blood liver and spleen. Flowing blood is impure. Liver and spleen as organs are distinguished from flowing or exuded blood; al-Tahtawi cites the lawful liver/spleen exception. [H5; H7] Blood retained in flesh and veins has separate details; purity of an organ does not purify blood flowing from it.

5 Invocation at slaughter. Al-Quduri makes intentional omission of Allah’s name disqualifying and excuses forgetful omission. [H4]

6 Transformation. Ibn Abidin presents complete change of substance as purifying in the selected view, associated with Muhammad and also Abu Hanifa; Abu Yusuf differs. [H1] The examples concern replacement of the underlying substance, such as salt or ash; school disagreement prevents a unanimity claim.

7 Processed ingredients. The transformation doctrine supplies a possible route for an originally impure input, but no serum/gelatin process ruling was found here. Rennet is itself disputed: Abu Hanifa regards non-pig carcass rennet as pure; Abu Yusuf and Muhammad treat contamination differently. [H1; H6]

8 Sea animals. Al-Quduri restricts aquatic edibility to fish and disapproves floating dead fish; al-Tahtawi states that fish require no slaughter. [H4; H7] The translation’s 'disapproved' must not be reduced to harmless dislike; exact tafi conditions and legal force require fuller commentary.

9 Living cells and the modern analogy. Classical detached-part and transformation rules create separate starting questions: lawful source at removal, then whether subsequent material remains the same legal substance. [H2; H1] The passages do not discuss cell lines, proliferation, differentiation or cultured meat. Juristic 'life' is not a cell-viability assay.

## Maliki source account

Nine bounded answers from the named sources. Modern applications remain inferences. Full qualifications and review decisions are retained in the school register.

1 Food categories and authority. Al-Qurtubi expressly attributes to Malik an interpretation of tayyibat as legally permitted things and khabaith as legally forbidden things; personal repulsion alone does not create the prohibition in this account. [M-QUR7157; M-BID-Q1]

2 Scope of the four prohibitions. A blanket four-items-only description fails: Malik's Muwatta transmits the prohibition of fanged predators and endorses the local practice. Ibn Rushd also reports a major Maliki reading classifying predators as disliked and explains its reconciliation with Quran 6:145. [M-MUW25; M-BID-FOOD]

3 Carcasses and detached parts. The Risala requires prescribed slaughter for relevant land animals and treats fatal injury as a carrion issue. Dardir/Dasuqi classify parts detached from a living animal whose carcass is impure as impure; the commentary explicitly includes flesh, sinews and veins. [M-RIS29; M-DAS30]

4 Blood liver and spleen. Al-Qarafi treats flowing blood as impure and non-flowing blood as pure on the sounder view. Dardir explains the latter as residual blood after lawful slaughter. Ibn Rushd notes the two-blood exception report but does not name its organs in the inspected passage. [M-DHAK; M-DAS28; M-BID-FOOD] Ibn Rushd records internal disagreement about non-flowing blood and fish blood. The liver/spleen permission is directly named in Hanbali source H-MUGH-LIVER, but that alone is not a verified Maliki textual statement.

5 Invocation at slaughter. The Risala permits eating when the invocation was forgotten, but rejects deliberate omission; it extends the distinction to sending trained hunting animals. [M-RIS29]

6 Transformation. Al-Qarafi distinguishes complete disappearance of impurity-defining qualities from partial change and records disagreement about carrion ash. Dardir/Dasuqi list vinegar conversion and musk among pure substances. These texts do not support an unqualified claim that all processing purifies. [M-DHAK; M-DAS28] Al-Qarafi distinguishes accepted wine-to-vinegar purification from disputed/rejected bone-to-ash cases. His reported consensus concerns the fully changed case as he defines it, not every modern chemical process.

7 Processed ingredients. No inspected classical text addresses industrial serum, isolated enzymes or gelatin manufacturing. The Risala distinguishes contaminated liquid from removable contamination in solid food; al-Qarafi records disagreement about meat cooked in impure water. [M-RIS29; M-DHAK]

8 Sea animals. Malik explicitly permits fish caught by Magians because marine carrion is edible. Ibn Rushd reports Malik's broad permission for sea animals, while Dardir treats marine carcasses as pure even when the animal survives on land. [M-MUW25; M-BID-FOOD; M-DAS28] Ibn Rushd preserves Malik's hesitation/dislike about the creature named sea-pig. Purity is also a distinct question from permission to eat every harmful specimen.

9 Living cells and the modern analogy. The detached-tissue rule in Q3 and transformation distinctions in Q6 provide competing lines of analysis. None of the inspected classical passages discusses a viable cell line, serial proliferation, differentiation or cultured edible biomass. [M-DAS30; M-DHAK] Biological viability does not by itself show that a detached part escapes its legal category; continued source ancestry does not by itself disprove a legally relevant transformation.

## Shafii source account

Nine bounded answers from the named sources. Modern applications remain inferences. Full qualifications and review decisions are retained in the school register.

1 Food categories and authority. Al-Nawawi’s discussion uses qualified Arab customary palatability for otherwise unclassified animals: settled, adequately supplied people, rather than desperate or indiscriminate eaters. [S1]

2 Scope of the four prohibitions. Reliance prohibits predators and harmful or impure foods in addition to the four-item formulation. The operative list therefore contains further restrictions. [S2; S1]

3 Carcasses and detached parts. Nawawi treats ordinary unslaughtered carcasses and living-detached parts as impure, with distinct carcass exceptions for fish, locust and humans. [S3]

4 Blood liver and spleen. Nawawi permits eating liver and spleen, but distinguishes blood exuding from those organs, judged impure on the stronger view. [S6; S5] Residual blood on meat/bones is separately discussed with hardship-based accommodation; organ edibility is not general blood permission.

5 Invocation at slaughter. Nawawi treats invocation as recommended: omission, deliberate or forgetful, does not invalidate slaughter. Deliberate omission is mildly disapproved in the preferred account. [S7]

6 Transformation. Nawawi’s preferred account rejects purification of intrinsically impure matter merely by becoming ash or salt. Wine becoming vinegar is distinguished. [S4] He records an internal permissive transformation view but rejects it as unsound; neither absolute unanimity nor 'no transformation ever' is accurate.

7 Processed ingredients. Read together, the impurity-consumption prohibition and restrictive transformation account do not establish that processed impure serum, enzymes or gelatin become permissible. [S2; S4]

8 Sea animals. Reliance permits aquatic game with listed exclusions including frogs and crocodiles, and exempts fish and locusts from slaughter, including natural death. [S2] This is the selected manual’s formulation, not proof that all amphibious species or every internal school opinion agree.

9 Living cells and the modern analogy. Detached-part impurity and restrictive transformation provide the closest verified classical issues for living-cell cultivation, rather than an explicit cultured-meat ruling. [S3; S4] Neither text decides whether a cell, cell line or differentiated biomass remains legally the original detached part.

## Hanbali source account

Nine bounded answers from the named sources. Modern applications remain inferences. Full qualifications and review decisions are retained in the school register.

1 Food categories and authority. Ibn Qudama's explanation of al-Khiraqi uses urban Hijazi food judgments for tayyib/khabith terminology, excluding necessity-driven Bedouin practice. Unfamiliar animals are compared with the closest Hijazi analogue; lacking an analogue, the general permission applies. [H-MUGH-FOOD; H-UM-FOOD2]

2 Scope of the four prohibitions. Umdat al-fiqh forbids fanged predators, taloned birds, domestic donkeys and specified other animals. Its non-animal-food opening excludes impurity and harm. The operative manual therefore extends beyond a four-item food blacklist. [H-UM-FOOD2; H-UM-FOOD]

3 Carcasses and detached parts. Umdat al-fiqh requires slaughter for relevant land animals. Al-Mughni treats what is cut from an animal retaining stable life as carrion because cutting is not slaughter; it distinguishes a part separated after effective slaughter. [H-MUGH-PARTS; H-UM-SLAUGHTER]

4 Blood liver and spleen. Al-Mardawi's al-Insaf distinguishes prohibited shed blood from residual blood in edible meat/veins, recording purity as the sounder position with dissent. It treats liver and spleen as pure; al-Mughni explicitly invokes their permission report. [H-INS-BLOOD; H-MUGH-LIVER] The al-Mughni passage is primarily about the word meat in oaths, not a complete blood taxonomy. Al-Insaf records residual-blood disagreement; the host's edition pagination needs verification.

5 Invocation at slaughter. Umdat al-fiqh's slaughter matn makes intentional omission disqualifying and excuses forgetfulness for the slaughtered animal. [H-UM-SLAUGHTER] The same matn treats hunting more strictly: forgotten invocation also prevents eating. A modern footnote records an alternative report. Do not transfer the ordinary-slaughter exception to every hunting case.

6 Transformation. Al-Mughni denies general purification by transformation. Al-Mubdi states the spontaneous wine-to-vinegar exception and records contrary reports; Ibn Taymiyya argues for purification after complete transformation, expressly distinguishing that preference from the familiar restrictive school account. [H-MUGH-IST; H-MUBDI; H-TAY-IST] Al-Mubdi also records disagreement over deliberately making vinegar. Ibn Taymiyya's broader preference must not be relabeled unanimous Hanbali doctrine.

7 Processed ingredients. Al-Mughni regards carrion milk and rennet as impure in the apparent school position, but reports a purity alternative. This supplies a concrete ingredient precedent; it does not describe serum fractionation, purified industrial enzymes or gelatin production. [H-MUGH-RENNET; H-MUBDI; H-TAY-IST]

8 Sea animals. Umdat al-fiqh permits sea animals without slaughter but requires slaughter for those also living on land, except crabs and similar creatures. Its food chapter separately excludes snakes, frogs and crocodiles from edible sea animals. [H-UM-SLAUGHTER; H-UM-FOOD2] Read the slaughter exemption together with the edible-species list. Neither all aquatic organisms are edible nor all amphibious organisms are slaughter-exempt follows from the first sentence alone.

9 Living cells and the modern analogy. Al-Mughni's stable-life excision rule is a relevant starting point for donor tissue. The inspected texts do not determine whether continuously viable cultured cells or their descendants remain that detached part or constitute a legally changed substance. [H-MUGH-PARTS; H-TAY-IST] Continued cellular life is a biological fact, while mayta in the excision rule is a legal classification. Neither viability nor multiplication alone answers whether the legal classification changes.

## Sistani source account

Nine bounded answers from the named sources. Modern applications remain inferences. Full qualifications and review decisions are retained in the school register.

1 Food categories and authority. An exact Sistani rule identifying who decides tayyib/khabith was not located. His general taqlid rule requires following a qualified jurist’s rulings. [J1; J10] General juristic authority and explicit impurity/harm rules do not answer the specific customary-repugnance test. No whole-school conclusion is warranted.

2 Scope of the four prohibitions. Sistani lists prohibited predatory birds and prohibited parts of otherwise lawful animals. The four-item formula is therefore not his complete operative dietary list. [J6]

3 Carcasses and detached parts. Unlawfully dead gush-blood animals are impure; flesh or life-containing parts detached while alive are impure. Wool, bones and other legally lifeless parts are distinguished. [J2]

4 Blood liver and spleen. Gush-blood animal blood is impure, with conditions for blood remaining after lawful slaughter and drainage. Sistani prohibits spleen among lawful land-animal parts. For birds, the other listed organs beyond blood and droppings are subject to obligatory precaution. [J3; J6] The Sunni paired liver/spleen exception cannot be transferred here. A direct affirmative liver ruling was not extracted; omission from a list alone is weaker evidence.

5 Invocation at slaughter. Invocation by the slaughterer is required. Sistani expressly excuses forgetting, whereas omission through ignorance makes the animal unlawful. [J5]

6 Transformation. True transformation of essence into a new pure thing purifies. Grinding impure wheat into flour or baking it does not; doubtful transformation leaves impurity. [J4] Some borderline changes receive precautionary treatment. A processing operation is not automatically a change of legal substance.

7 Processed ingredients. Official gelatin rulings distinguish unknown source, known animal origin, lawful slaughter, minute absorption and established transformation. Arabic answers qualify that manufacturing transformation is not established and assess identity by customary understanding. [J9; J8; J2; J3] Minute absorption is not a general permission for known impure gelatin. English chemical-change wording does not establish every hydrolysis as transformation. This account does not resolve every enzyme or serum preparation.

8 Sea animals. Scaled fish require lawful capture conditions rather than land-animal slaughter; death in water generally disqualifies, with a net-capture exception. Scaleless fish are prohibited. [J7] The fisher need not be Muslim or invoke Allah’s name, but capture must be established. Pure fish carcass does not mean edible fish carcass.

9 Living cells and the modern analogy. The verified framework separates a living-detached source from genuine later transformation; no official cultivated-cell ruling was established in this extraction. [J2; J4; J7] Fishing rules distinguish pieces removed from a live fish, showing that mammalian detached-part analysis cannot simply cover every species.
